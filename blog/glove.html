GloVe: learning vectors from co-occurrence

What the objective fits—and why vector arithmetic is an illustration, not a guarantee.

Yasaman Samiee · Updated September 17, 2026 · 1 min read

GloVe learns word representations from aggregate word-context co-occurrence statistics. A context window defines which word pairs contribute counts. The model fits a weighted objective relating a word-context dot product and bias terms to the logarithm of a nonzero co-occurrence count.

Why weighting matters

Very rare co-occurrences are unreliable estimates. The weighting function reduces their influence and saturates for sufficiently frequent pairs. Separate word and context vectors allow the model to capture their different roles in the objective.

What not to infer from a count

Words that often appear together need not have similar meanings: opposites and complementary terms can share contexts. Nor does each vector dimension correspond to an interpretable concept. Analogy arithmetic can illustrate patterns learned from a corpus, but does not guarantee reliable reasoning.

Unlike contextual representations, a standard GloVe lookup supplies one vector per vocabulary item. The same word used with different meanings receives the same stored vector. Corpus choice and coverage therefore matter when evaluating these features for a downstream task.

Original project and paper: Stanford GloVe.
