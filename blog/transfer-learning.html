Transfer learning: freeze first, then decide what to adapt

A practical distinction between using learned features and fine-tuning them.

Yasaman Samiee · Updated September 17, 2026 · 1 min read

Transfer learning reuses information learned on one task for another. In a neural network, this often means starting with pretrained weights instead of training every parameter from scratch.

Two useful starting points

With a frozen feature extractor, keep the pretrained backbone fixed and train a new task-specific head. With fine-tuning, update some or all backbone weights as well. Fine-tuning is not limited to a particular layer; the choice depends on the data, task, and compute budget.

A concrete decision

For a small labeled image dataset, compare a frozen backbone plus classifier against a cautiously fine-tuned version on the same held-out split. If the images differ strongly from the pretraining domain, frozen features may be insufficient. If labels are scarce, updating many parameters may overfit.

Check input normalization, label definitions, and overlap between training and evaluation data. Pretraining reduces the amount of learning needed; it does not guarantee that the learned features transfer. Keep a simple baseline to measure whether the extra complexity helps.
