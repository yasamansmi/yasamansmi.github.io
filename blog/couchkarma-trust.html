How CouchKarma picks a movie

The streaming checks behind a recommendation, and what happens when they fail.

Yasaman Samiee · Updated September 17, 2026 · 2 min read

Say you want a short comedy and you have Netflix. A recommendation that turns out to cost $5 to rent elsewhere hasn’t really answered the request.

That’s one of the details I had to account for in CouchKarma, an app I’m building to help people find something to watch, read, or listen to. You give it your mood, available time, and preferences. For movies and series, you can also choose your streaming subscriptions.

Start with movies the app can check

Before asking the AI to pick a movie, the app gathers a list of candidates and checks their streaming availability in Canada. If you’ve selected subscriptions, the candidates need to be included in those services. A rental listing doesn’t count.

The AI gets that list and chooses an ID. The server then looks up the matching record for the title, runtime, and links. Those fields come from the record; the model writes the short explanation of why it chose the movie.

I kept that separation because streaming availability is something the app can look up. There’s no reason to ask the model to guess it.

When a lookup fails

A failed lookup for one movie shouldn’t stop the whole search. The app skips that movie and continues with candidates it could check.

But if the availability checks fail and leave no usable candidates, it returns an error and asks you to try again. It doesn’t quietly remove your subscription choices to produce an answer.

There’s also a less dramatic possibility: the checks worked, but nothing in the batch fits. That’s a reason to try different preferences, not proof that there’s no suitable movie anywhere.

Would you actually enjoy the pick?

That’s the part I haven’t measured across users yet. The code checks whether a choice comes from the supplied list and whether it meets the streaming requirements. It can still return a movie you don’t want to watch, with an explanation that misses what you meant.

For a broader evaluation, I’d track those separately: did the app respect the request, and did the person like the recommendation? The first is easier to test. The second needs feedback from people using it.
